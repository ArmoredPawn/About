# Statecraft

A real-time geopolitics game for up to 20 leaders. Each player is handed one of the world's 50 largest economies at
random; the computer runs the other countries, and every other nation on Earth (about 150 more) is a full country it
runs too, with its own economy, army, navy and diplomacy. No alliances and no oil cartel exist at the start. The world never pauses: a week passes
every few seconds, armies glide across the map and front lines creep through contested provinces as you watch.

- **Economy:** taxes, six spending lines, the interest rate, debt, inflation, stimulus, rationing.
- **Government:** conscription (volunteers to a total-war draft), the security forces, the press, elections, martial
  law. People protest for real reasons (school, hospital or pension cuts, prices, shortages, a war or a draft they
  don't believe in), and fear, propaganda and a recovering economy keep them home. Unanswered, protests become
  uprisings and civil war; a country in civil war makes a separate peace abroad, as Russia did at Brest-Litovsk.
- **Resources where they really are:** oil (Texas, Alberta, West Siberia, the Gulf, the North Caucasus…), gas, coal,
  iron, grain, rare earths, uranium, critical metals and Taiwan's chip fabs. Capture a province and its output, and
  its people, are yours. Empty deserts (Iran's Kavir and Lut, Arabia's Empty Quarter, the Outback, the Thar) are
  provinces of their own with little worth taking.
- **Market:** buy from the country you choose, or around sanctions through intermediaries; sell surplus or list it
  for other leaders. Every cargo sails through the real straits or goes overland, and can be seized or sunk on the
  way, with both sides told who did it. Hold a strait's shores to close it, charge a toll or attack enemy cargoes. Send any fleet that can reach a cargo (best first) or a new one from the nearest port; fleets can hunt shipping across a whole sea, seizing or sinking as each is set to.
- **Navies:** every fleet sits at its exact spot on the water. Form one at any of your ports, click it, then click
  anywhere on the sea: it sails the shortest way round the land (through the straits and canals) to hold position,
  blockade an enemy's coast or patrol a strait to escort friends or stop everyone else. Enemy fleets that come within
  about 450 km fight; a beaten fleet runs into port to refit. Landing an army overseas needs a fleet lying off that
  coast, at least as strong as the enemy's there.
- **Peoples:** every province has a people and a loyalty to whoever rules it. Conquered peoples resist with partisans
  and uprisings that can hand a province back; real independence movements (Catalonia, Scotland, Quebec, Kashmir,
  Tibet, Kurdistan, Balochistan…) can be granted autonomy, cracked down on or offered a referendum. Wars drive
  refugees across borders, which neighbours take in or turn away.
- **Oil and gas:** no cartel exists at the start; producers can found one that sets a production cut and embargoes a
  country, as OPEC did in 1973; members can cheat on their
  quota and get caught. Real pipelines (Druzhba, Power of Siberia, Petroline round Hormuz…) deliver whatever happens
  at sea, can be shut as leverage or sabotaged, and planned ones (Power of Siberia 2, EastMed, TAPI…) can be built.
  Strategic reserves can be released in a price shock.
- **Climate:** CO2 from every country's oil, gas and coal warms the world (0.45 °C per thousand billion tonnes).
  Warming brings heatwaves, floods, wildfires, storms and failing harvests in hot, dry lands. Carbon taxes and
  pledges made at five-yearly climate summits, kept or broken.
- **Space:** real 2027 satellite fleets (GPS, BeiDou, GLONASS, Galileo, spy and communications satellites) that let
  a country see every army on Earth, guide its missiles and keep its forces organised; anti-satellite strikes and
  debris; and the race to crewed flight, a space station, the Moon and Mars.
- **Money and markets:** every country's currency, stock market and credit rating, moved by interest rates,
  inflation, debt, stability, war, sanctions and the world's mood, and feeding back into inflation, growth, approval
  and borrowing costs. Central-bank intervention and capital controls. Debt crises end in an IMF programme (with
  conditions that are checked every quarter), austerity, printing money or default.
- **Global crises:** pandemics that spread along borders and trade routes (lockdowns, border closures, a vaccine
  race the funders win, and the choice to share it or hoard it); financial crashes (bank rescues, an emergency
  summit and a coordinated stimulus that rewards joiners and tempts free-riders); famines and food aid; terror
  attacks and how to answer them. Leaders face each choice as a decision with a deadline.
- **Coups and regime change:** army loyalty and coup risk (instability, poverty, unpaid or beaten armies, unpopular
  governments, foreign money), plots to pay off or purge, military juntas (including the Sahel's and Myanmar's in
  2027), revolutions against hated dictatorships, coups backed through spy networks, and regime change as a peace
  term.
- **Proxy wars:** arm and fund another country's war, or rebels in someone else's, openly or covertly.
- **War, on a front line:** provinces are what the economy counts (output, resources, cities, garrisons, forts);
  the ground between them is a grid of 50 km cells that change hands as armies advance, so a front runs through
  the middle of a province and each side gets the output of what it holds. Armies stand at exact points. Click one,
  then click anywhere: our ground to march there and hold it, enemy ground to advance and take it, an enemy army to
  attack it. Only armies hold ground: land with no army on it is marched through (slowed by its garrison and home
  guard), and a battle happens only when an army that means to attack meets an enemy army, so two countries can be at
  war for months without a shot (a phoney war). The loser of a battle is pushed back and the winner takes the ground
  between; overwhelming odds shatter an army. Declare war in one click (leaving any alliance you share) once its case
  is made; armies cross other countries only with their permission. Air missions, missile strikes, blockades,
  landings, nuclear weapons, peace treaties and armistices.
- **Encirclement:** every province draws supply over friendly ground from the capital and heartland. Cut the last path
  and the forces beyond are a pocket: organisation, fuel and men drain every week until they break out, are relieved,
  are kept alive by an airlift (as at Stalingrad) or surrender. Coastal pockets whose waters the enemy doesn't hold are
  supplied by sea. Attacks from two sides are pincers; mechanised armies break through, bypass garrisons and keep
  rolling. Invaded peoples raise a home guard, and big cities in the front's path mean street fighting. Defenders hold
  the line: computer-run armies (and yours, with "Support the front") move in to defend a province under attack next
  door, fall back onto the new line when a province falls, and march up wherever the front has moved on.
- **Cities:** over 7,000 cities and towns, dozens in every country, each with its share of its province's output.
- **Doctrine** (Military → Doctrine): build the way the army fights from five choices (attack, defence, air power,
  logistics, command), develop and train it in, and master it in battle. Each attack concept fares better against some
  defences than others; mastered combinations become new ways of war (Blitzkrieg, Deep operations, AirLand Battle…),
  and the first country to develop one is its pioneer.
- **Planned cargo routes** (Economy → Trade): the seller of a resource deal plans its convoys' route with up to 10
  checkpoints on the map (straight lines between them, never over land by sea or over the sea by truck), or lets the
  computer find the shortest, then sees what shipping costs per convoy and over the deal before going ahead. In a
  private deal the seller names the price. A public cargo and its route are on every map; a private one is seen only
  by the two countries, spies inside either, spy satellites, and warships that come very close (their leader can
  seize it, sink it, broadcast it or let it pass). Neutral countries whose spies find a secret trade may broadcast it;
  a sinking is heard by spies and satellites.
- **Design bureau** (Research → Design): design your own tanks, armoured vehicles, aircraft and warships part by part
  on a blueprint, name them and have the factories build them; each design's rating changes how good that arm is.
- **Supplies and supply lines:** every army eats fuel, food and munitions; beyond its borders it lives at the end of a
  supply line that costs money each week and that the enemy can bomb. With nothing getting through, it lasts two weeks,
  then falls apart fast.
- **The war bar** (bottom of the map): air strikes, missile strikes (fear, and nationalism), amphibious landings and
  cutting supply lines, one click each. Spies inside an enemy show its output and troops while you aim.
- **Spies and satellites:** a network inside a country shows its designs, army numbers and supplies, stockpiles, war
  plans, secret trade and where some of its fleets are going; each spy satellite counts a share of the world's
  armies and fleets exactly.
- **Computer-run countries** trade, sign deals and pacts (some secret), spy on rivals and pick their own fights with
  weak neighbours. The smaller nations found regional trade blocs (the East African Community, the Caribbean
  Community…), ask friendly great powers for protection against a threatening rival, and fight old feuds (Armenia and
  Azerbaijan, Serbia and Kosovo, Venezuela and Guyana…). Invaded, they mobilise, conscript, draft every reservist, fortify, gather their armies for counter-offensives and call on their friends; allies send expeditionary armies to the front.
- **Diplomacy:** public or secret alliances and trade blocs, free trade, non-aggression pacts, military access, resource
  deals, loans, money aid that arrives at once, sanctions, tariffs, UN resolutions with P5 vetoes. An alliance with
  one other country is one proposal. Free trade, non-aggression pacts, military access, loans, alliances, resource
  deals and market purchases can all be public or secret: a secret one is known only to the two sides and spies
  inside either, and neutral spies who find it may broadcast it (secret military access comes out when the troops do).
- **Spies:** plant agents abroad; once settled in they relay armies on the move, weapons being built, secret pacts and
  war plans, and show the target's armies on your map, until counter-intelligence catches (or turns) them.
- **Research:** pay for a project and wait for it; the hardest work needs imported chips or uranium, or buy a licence
  from a country further ahead.
- **News:** everything the world has seen, searchable by country. Secret moves never appear there.
- **Chat:** a world channel, one-to-one messages, group chats, and a chat for every alliance.
- **Solo play** runs entirely in the browser and saves as you go.

Figures are rounded real-world values from around 2025 (IMF, IISS, SIPRI, FAS, USGS, EIA). The map is Natural Earth; the cities are Natural Earth and GeoNames (CC BY 4.0).

## How it fits together

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The game (`public/`) | GitHub Pages, as `statecraft.html` on the site | The interface, plus solo play |
| The engine (`public/js/engine/`) | Both | The whole simulation, shared by the server and solo play |
| Multiplayer server (`server.js`, `lib/`) | Render | Runs rooms of up to 20 and checks every command |

The engine is plain JavaScript with no dependencies, so the same code runs in the browser and on the server.

## Playing it on this computer

```sh
npm install
npm run dev   # http://localhost:3001 (multiplayer and solo)
```

`npm test` runs the tests.

Multiplayer updates are kept small: each weekly update carries only the fields of other countries that changed since
that connection's last update.

## Changing the world

- Countries: `public/js/engine/data/countries.js`. Provinces and their resources: `data/regions.js`.
  The smaller nations: their size, people, forces and resources in `data/minors.js`; flags, governments, feuds,
  claims and trade blocs in `data/nations.js`. Starting friendships, rivalries, sanctions and straits: `data/world.js`.
- The sea fleets sail on (and the open water off every coastal province) is generated by `npm run build:seas`.
- The land grid armies fight over (data/land.js) is generated from the province outlines by `npm run build:land`;
  run it after `npm run build:map`.
- After changing provinces or their seed points, run `npm run build:map`. It rebuilds `public/data/map.json` and
  `public/js/engine/data/geo.js` (both committed) and warns about any province that ends up with no land.

## Putting multiplayer online (one time)

1. https://dashboard.render.com > **New > Blueprint**, connect the `ArmoredPawn/About` repository; it finds
   `Statecraft/render.yaml`. (Or **New > Web Service** with **Root Directory** `Statecraft`, **Build Command**
   `npm install`, **Start Command** `npm start`, **Health Check Path** `/healthz`, **Instance Type** Free, and the
   environment variable `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` = `https://armoredpawn.github.io`.)
2. Once it's live, open `https://<your-service>.onrender.com/healthz`; it should show `"ok":true`.
3. Put that address in `public/js/config.js` as `serverUrl`, commit and push.

On the free plan the server sleeps after 15 minutes without use and takes about a minute to wake; the game says so
while it waits. Games live in the server's memory, so a restart ends any game in progress. Solo play never needs the server.
