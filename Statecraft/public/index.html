    How to play

    You lead one of the 50 largest economies. The world runs in real time and never pauses: armies move, fronts shift and the date ticks forward day by day, whether you act or not. There is no time limit: the game ends only when one country has conquered the entire world.

    Run the country

    • Economy: set taxes, spending and the interest rate. Infrastructure, education and research grow the economy; taxes and high interest rates slow it. Deficits pile up debt.
    • Government: conscription, the security forces, the press and the home front. People protest for real reasons (school or hospital cuts, prices, a war or a draft they don't believe in), and how many take to the streets depends on fear and belief as much as anger. Give in, negotiate or send the police. Ignored, a fragile country can slide into uprisings and civil war.
    • Conscription needs a reason: with an enemy at the gates people accept it; with none, expect an outcry.
    • Market: buy from the country of your choice (or around sanctions, through intermediaries), sell your surplus, and watch your cargoes travel by sea or land. Anyone can try to stop them, and both sides are told who did.
    • Research: fund a project, wait for the scientists, and import the chips or uranium the hardest work needs, or buy a licence from a country further ahead.
    • Peoples (Govern → Peoples): every province has a people and a loyalty to whoever rules it. Conquered peoples resist for years, with partisans and uprisings; movements for independence (Catalonia, Scotland, Kashmir, Tibet, Kurdistan…) can be granted autonomy, cracked down on, or offered a referendum. Wars drive refugees across borders, which you can take in or turn away.
    • Oil & pipelines (Market): OPEC+ cuts output to raise prices and can embargo a country, as in 1973; members can quietly cheat. Pipelines deliver whatever happens at sea, and can be shut as leverage or sabotaged. Release your strategic reserve in a price shock.
    • Climate (World → Climate): every tonne of CO2 burned warms the world for good. Warming brings heatwaves, floods, wildfires, storms and failing harvests in hot, dry lands. Tax carbon and pledge cuts at the climate summits held every five years: kept pledges win standing, broken ones cost more.
    • Space (Research → Space): four spy satellites show every army and fleet in exact numbers; your own navigation satellites (six or more) make missiles and attacks hit harder; communications satellites keep forces organised. Race for the Moon and Mars, or shoot down an enemy's satellites and fill orbit with debris.

    War: you plan, your generals fight

    • Declare war from a country's dossier or any of its provinces. If you share an alliance with it, you leave in the same click.
    • Draft & form an army group (Military → Troops): choose how many troops (more than you have free drafts reservists) and the province they form up in. The top of the Military tab always shows how many you can deploy.
    • Click your army, then click anywhere on the map: it goes there at once. It marches through friendly ground, fights its way through enemy ground, and lands from the sea when there's no way over land. If it can't get there, the bar at the bottom of the map says why.
    • Fronts move both ways: an attack that's thrown back invites a counter-attack into the ground it came from.
    • Make the case for war (Govern → Home front, or the box in the war dialog) before you strike: a war the nation has been told is necessary meets far less outcry and fewer anti-war protests.
    • Watch it happen: armies glide across the map and a yellow front line creeps through contested provinces. Countries at war with you are outlined in red. Every army is on everyone's map, but other leaders only see rough numbers unless their spies are deep inside your government. When an attack stalls or is thrown back, the News says why: how many defenders are in the way and the odds.
    • How fast armies march depends on Land warfare and Industry research, how many tanks they have per thousand men, and oil: from about 300 km a week for low-tech infantry to 1,100 km for a modern mechanised army. Mountains and forests slow them. In battle, numbers count most: you need better than even odds to advance, an even fight takes about a month, and a 3:1 attack about a week. Tired, badly supplied armies fight worse, but never so badly that a much bigger army loses to a small one on open ground.
    • Your troops can cross another country only if it grants military access; otherwise you must fight through it.
    • Check the ground: Ground & passes on a province shows forests, mountains, marshes and passes. Ground thought impassable, like the Ardennes, is lightly held: attack through it before it's fortified and the defenders are caught off guard.
    • Wars far from home need money, factories and men to guard the supply lines (Military → Logistics). Soldiers who die are gone from the population; conquered land's people become yours.
    • Aircraft and missiles only reach so far from your bases (or an ally's); extend their range from Military → Weapons.
    • Navies are fleets on the map (Military → Navy). Click a fleet, then click a sea, a strait (◆) or a coast: it sails there and holds the sea, blockades an enemy's coast, or patrols the strait to escort friendly ships or stop others. Enemy fleets in the same waters fight; a beaten fleet runs for home or into port. To land an army overseas you need a fleet in the sea off that coast, at least as strong as the enemy's there.
    • Hold a strait's shores to close it, charge a toll or attack enemy cargoes.
    • Computer-run countries fight back with everything when invaded, and some plan wars of their own.

    Diplomacy and spies

    • Message other leaders, or start group chats. Every alliance gets its own chat. Alliances can be secret: only members, and spies inside them, know.
    • Propose alliances, free trade, non-aggression pacts, military access, resource deals and loans. Impose sanctions and tariffs, or table UN resolutions.
    • Arm & fund (Diplomacy): send weapons, money or volunteers to a country at war, or to rebels in someone else's country, openly or covertly. Covert help costs more and can be exposed.
    • Spies: plant agents abroad and they relay armies on the move, weapons being built, secret pacts and war plans until counter-intelligence catches them. Run your own counter-intelligence to catch theirs.
    • News keeps everything the world has seen; pick a country to read all about it. Secret moves never appear there.

    Map controls

    Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Click a province for details and actions; click an army or a fleet, then click where it should go (Esc cancels). The buttons at the top switch map modes (including Loyalty and Climate) and show or hide armies, fleets and pipelines.
