    How to play

    You lead one of the 50 largest economies. Time runs a week at a time: every few seconds a new week begins and the world moves on, whether you act or not. The game ends after the number of years the host chose, and countries are ranked by power, growth and territory.

    Run the country

    • Economy: set taxes, spending and the interest rate. Infrastructure, education and research grow the economy; taxes and high interest rates slow it. Deficits pile up debt, and too much debt raises borrowing costs.
    • Resources sit where they really are: oil in Texas, Alberta, West Siberia and the Gulf; gas in Qatar, Yamal and South Pars; rare earths in Inner Mongolia; chips in Taiwan and Korea. Shortages slow the economy and stall armies; click a province to develop new fields and mines.
    • Approval and stability follow growth, prices, taxes, public services and war. Democracies face elections; unstable countries face protests and crises.

    War: you plan, your generals fight

    • Form army groups from your forces, then give each an operation: attack a province (the general finds the route and fights the battles on the way), hold a line, or make an amphibious landing.
    • Set how aggressive each general should be. Generals regroup when worn out and wait for better odds when cautious.
    • Use air campaigns, missile strikes and naval blockades. Cut an enemy off from oil and its tanks stop.
    • Wars end with a peace treaty: cede provinces, pay reparations, or freeze the front with an armistice.

    Diplomacy

    • Message other leaders, or start group chats. Every alliance gets its own chat.
    • Propose alliances, free trade, non-aggression pacts, military access, resource deals and loans. Impose sanctions and tariffs, close straits, table UN resolutions, or run covert operations.
    • Attacking without a claim costs world standing and invites sanctions; attacking an alliance member brings its allies in.

    Map controls

    Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Click a province for details and actions; click an army to command it. Map modes show alliances, resources and the economy.
